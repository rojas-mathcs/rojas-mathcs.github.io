Maurice's A-Discriminant Page

The A-discriminant (derived by Israel Moseyevich Gelfand, Misha Kapranov, and Andrei Zelevinsky around the 1980's) is a deep algebraic invariant attached to a point set A in Z^n.

The A-discriminant includes all toric resultants (including the classical ``dense'' resultants of Sylvester and Cayley) as special cases. Furthermore, its real zero set separates coefficient space into chambers on which the topology of certain families of real algebraic sets remains constant up to isotopy.

To illustrate the latter point, one can consider equations of the form (x^{2d}+ay^d+y,y^{2d}+bx^d+x) and try to find when such equations have maximally many roots in an orthant. This question is intimately related to the failure of Kushnirenko's Conjecture from real algebraic geometry (real fewnomial theory, to be precise).

In particular, a conjectured bound of Kushnirenko puts the maximal number of roots of any such system (for any (a,b,d)) at 4. Kushnirenko has stated that he knew his conjecture was wrong shortly after he formulated it, and a simple counter-example was found by a colleague of his in Moscow. However, no one appears to have recorded this counter-example or to remember who its original author was.

Bertrand Haas, in 2000, found a simple counter-example with d=54 and (in our notation), a and b easily expressible algebraic numbers.

More recently, in joint work of Rojas, Rusek, and Shih, an even simpler counter-example was found: (a,b,d)=(44/31,44/31,3).
